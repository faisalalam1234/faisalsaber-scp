What Are After Effects Presets and How Do You Install Them?

Faisal Saber May 2026 4 min read After Effects · Beginner

If you are new to Adobe After Effects, you have probably heard the word "preset" and wondered what it actually means. Presets are one of the most powerful time-saving tools available in After Effects — once you understand how they work, they will fundamentally change how fast you can produce professional-quality edits. This guide explains everything from scratch.

What Exactly is an After Effects Preset?

An After Effects preset is a saved file (usually with the .ffx extension) that stores a complete set of effects, keyframes, expressions, and animation settings. When you apply a preset to a layer in After Effects, all those settings are instantly added to your layer — no manual setup required.

Think of it like a recipe. Instead of measuring every ingredient yourself every time you cook, you follow a recipe that has the perfect measurements already figured out. A preset is the same idea — someone has already set up all the parameters perfectly, and you just apply it to your footage.

Presets can contain almost anything: a simple color effect, a complex multi-layer animation, camera shake expressions, slow-motion settings, text animation sequences, transition effects, and much more.

Types of After Effects Presets

  • Animation Presets (.ffx) — The most common type. These apply effects and keyframes directly to a selected layer. This is what most free preset packs use, including all the presets on this platform.
  • Project Files (.aep) — Full After Effects project files that contain pre-built compositions, animations, and setups. You open these like a regular project and use the elements inside.
  • Motion Bro Presets — These require the free Motion Bro extension plugin. They provide a library-style panel for organizing and previewing large preset collections. Popular with professional editors.
  • Template Files (.mogrt) — Motion Graphics Templates that work in both Premiere Pro and After Effects. These are highly customizable and often used for title cards, lower thirds, and end screens.

Step-by-Step: How to Install .ffx Presets in After Effects

Step 1 — Download and Extract

Download the preset pack from our Resource Vault. You will receive a .zip file. Extract it by right-clicking and selecting "Extract All" on Windows or double-clicking on Mac. You should see individual .ffx files inside the extracted folder.

Step 2 — Find the User Presets Folder

You need to place your .ffx files in a specific folder so After Effects can find them. The location is:

Windows: C:\Users\[YourName]\Documents\Adobe\After Effects [version]\User Presets\ Mac: /Users/[YourName]/Documents/Adobe/After Effects [version]/User Presets/

If the "User Presets" folder does not exist yet, create it yourself — just make sure the name is exactly "User Presets".

Step 3 — Copy Your .ffx Files

Copy all your .ffx preset files and paste them into the User Presets folder you found in Step 2. You can also create sub-folders inside User Presets to organise presets by type — for example "Shake Presets", "Twixtor Presets", "Transition Presets" etc.

Step 4 — Restart After Effects

Close After Effects completely if it is already open, then restart it. This is important — After Effects only scans for presets on startup.

Step 5 — Open the Effects & Presets Panel

Go to Window → Effects & Presets. The panel will open on the right side of your workspace. Scroll down to find "User Presets" — your installed presets will be listed there.

Step 6 — Apply a Preset

Select a layer in your timeline (click on it). Then in the Effects & Presets panel, double-click the preset you want to apply. The preset's effects and keyframes will instantly be added to your selected layer. That's it!

💡 Quick Tip — Drag and Drop

You can also apply presets by dragging them directly from the Effects & Presets panel and dropping them onto a layer in your timeline. This is sometimes faster than double-clicking, especially when your layer is not currently selected.

Common Installation Problems and Fixes

  • Preset not showing up — Make sure you fully restarted After Effects after copying the files. Also double-check you placed the .ffx files in the correct "User Presets" folder for your version of After Effects.
  • Missing effects error after applying preset — Some presets require specific plugins (like Twixtor, Element 3D, or Video Copilot plugins) to be installed. If you see an error about a missing effect, you need to install that plugin separately.
  • Preset applies but nothing looks different — This can happen if the preset uses expressions tied to specific layer names or null objects. Check if there are any instructions included with the preset pack.

How to Browse and Preview Presets

After Effects also has a built-in Adobe Bridge integration for browsing presets with visual previews. Go to Animation → Browse Presets to open Adobe Bridge. From there you can see small animated previews of each preset before applying it, which is very helpful when you have a large collection.
