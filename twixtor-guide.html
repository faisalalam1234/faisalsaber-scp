How to Use Twixtor Presets in After Effects — Complete Beginner Guide

Faisal Saber June 2026 5 min read After Effects · Slow Motion

Twixtor is one of the most powerful slow-motion tools available for video editors. Whether you want buttery-smooth cinematic slow motion or a dramatic velocity ramp that syncs perfectly to a beat drop, Twixtor presets can save you hours of manual keyframing. In this guide, we walk you through everything you need to know — from installation to applying presets to your footage.

What is Twixtor?

Twixtor is a plugin made by RE:Vision Effects that uses advanced optical flow technology to create ultra-smooth slow-motion effects. Unlike standard frame blending in After Effects, Twixtor actually synthesizes new frames by analyzing the motion between existing ones — producing a result that looks fluid and professional even at extreme slow speeds.

Twixtor presets are pre-configured settings files (.ffx) that automatically apply specific speed curves, motion blur settings, and timing to your footage. Instead of manually setting every parameter, you apply the preset and it handles everything in seconds.

What You Need Before You Start

  • Adobe After Effects — CS6 or any newer version
  • Twixtor plugin — Available from RE:Vision Effects (free trial available)
  • High frame rate footage — 60fps, 120fps, or 240fps recommended
  • Twixtor preset files (.ffx) — Download our free pack from the Resource Vault on the homepage
💡 Pro Tip — Frame Rate Matters

Twixtor produces the best results with footage shot at 60fps or higher. The more source frames your video has, the smoother the synthesized slow motion will look. 120fps or 240fps footage gives you the most cinematic results.

Step-by-Step: How to Install Twixtor Presets

Step 1

Download the Twixtor preset pack from our Resource Vault. You will receive a .zip file containing multiple .ffx preset files such as "Smooth Twixtor.ffx", "Velocity Twixtor.ffx", and more.

Step 2

Extract the .zip file. You should now have a folder with individual .ffx files inside it.

Step 3

Copy all the .ffx files into your After Effects User Presets folder. The default location depends on your operating system:

Windows: C:\Users\YourName\Documents\Adobe\After Effects [version]\User Presets\ Mac: ~/Documents/Adobe/After Effects [version]/User Presets/
Step 4

Restart Adobe After Effects. Open the Effects & Presets panel (go to Window → Effects & Presets). You will now see your Twixtor presets listed under the "User Presets" section.

How to Apply a Twixtor Preset to Your Footage

Step 1

Import your video footage into After Effects. Go to File → Import → File and select your video clip. Then drag it onto a new composition in the timeline.

Step 2

Click on your footage layer in the timeline to select it.

Step 3

In the Effects & Presets panel, locate your desired preset under User Presets. Double-click the preset (for example, "Smooth Twixtor") to apply it. After Effects will automatically add the Twixtor effect with all the preset's settings to your selected layer.

Step 4

Hit the Spacebar or press 0 on the numpad to RAM preview your clip. Your footage should now play back in slow motion with the preset's configuration applied.

Which Preset Should You Use?

Each preset in our free Twixtor pack is designed for a specific editing style. Here is a quick guide to help you choose the right one:

  • Smooth Twixtor — Best for cinematic, natural-feeling slow motion. Good for travel vlogs and cinematic edits.
  • Slow Twixtor — Deep slow motion at maximum stretch. Use for dramatic reveals and emotional moments.
  • Fast Twixtor — Quick velocity ramp. Great for high-energy sports and action sequences.
  • Velocity Twixtor — Gradually ramps from normal speed into slow motion. Perfect for beat sync edits.
  • Microwave Twixtor — The popular stuttered slow-motion style seen in AMV and gaming edits. Very trendy in 2026.
  • Cinematic Twixtor — Film-grade slow motion with smooth easing curves. Looks closest to professional film camera footage.
💡 Twixtor Rendering Tip

Twixtor is a processor-heavy effect. When previewing, set your composition resolution to Half or Quarter in the bottom left of the preview window. Only switch to Full resolution when you are ready to do your final render — this will save you a lot of time.

Common Twixtor Problems and How to Fix Them

Ghosting / Smearing Effect

If your slow motion footage has ghosting or blurry trails, it usually means Twixtor is having difficulty tracking motion between frames. Fix this by increasing the "Motion Sensitivity" value in the Twixtor effect controls, or by using higher frame rate source footage.

The Preset Is Not Showing in Effects & Presets

Make sure you placed the .ffx files in the correct User Presets folder and that you fully restarted After Effects (not just refreshed). Also check that the Twixtor plugin itself is installed — the preset requires the plugin to be present to work correctly.

Footage Looks Choppy Instead of Smooth

This usually happens when your source footage is 24fps or 30fps. Twixtor works best with 60fps+ footage. Consider shooting at a higher frame rate if your camera supports it.
