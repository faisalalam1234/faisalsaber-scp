How to Write AI Prompts for Cinematic Visuals — Midjourney & Sora Guide

Faisal Saber June 2026 8 min read AI Tools · Midjourney

AI image and video generation tools have fundamentally changed what is possible for solo video editors and content creators in 2026. With Midjourney, Sora, and similar tools, you can generate cinematic visuals, custom YouTube thumbnails, abstract background footage, and even short video sequences — without a camera, a set, or a production budget.

But here is the truth: most people using these tools get mediocre results because they do not know how to write effective prompts. The difference between a generic-looking AI image and a genuinely cinematic, professional-quality result usually comes down entirely to the quality of the prompt. This guide teaches you exactly how to write prompts that produce stunning cinematic visuals.

The Anatomy of a Great Cinematic AI Prompt

Think of an AI image prompt like a brief for a film director of photography. The more specific and visual your language is, the better the output will be. A strong cinematic prompt contains most or all of these elements:

Subject

Who or what is the main focus? Be specific — not just "a person" but "a young woman in a leather jacket"

Environment

Where is it? What is around? "neon-lit alley", "foggy mountain peak", "abandoned warehouse"

Camera Style

What kind of shot? "close-up portrait", "wide establishing shot", "low-angle drone view"

Lighting

How is it lit? "golden hour backlight", "blue moonlight", "dramatic single key light"

Color Palette

What are the dominant colors? "teal and orange", "monochromatic blue", "warm amber and deep shadow"

Film Style

"shot on 35mm film", "IMAX quality", "cinematic anamorphic lens", "anime cel shading"

Mood / Tone

"melancholic", "tense and foreboding", "euphoric", "mysterious and ethereal"

Quality Tags

"8K", "ultra detailed", "sharp focus", "depth of field", "photorealistic", "award-winning photography"

Midjourney-Specific Tips

  • --ar 16:9 — Always add this for widescreen results perfect for video thumbnails and backgrounds
  • --ar 9:16 — Use this for vertical format content (Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts)
  • --style raw — Produces more photorealistic, less "AI-looking" images
  • --v 6 — Use the latest version for the best quality results
  • --q 2 — Higher quality rendering (takes longer but produces better results)
  • --no text, watermark, logo — Use the --no parameter to exclude elements you do not want
💡 The Most Common Prompt Mistake

Writing prompts that are too short and vague. "A city at night" will produce a generic result. "Street-level view of a rain-soaked neon-lit cyberpunk city at midnight, reflected light in puddles, cinematic teal and orange color grade, 35mm film" will produce something remarkable. Add detail everywhere — the AI rewards specificity.

Using AI Prompts for YouTube Thumbnails

AI-generated images are excellent for custom YouTube thumbnails, especially for cinematic or editorial content. For thumbnails specifically, make sure your prompt emphasizes:

  • High contrast and bold colors — Thumbnails need to stand out at very small sizes in a crowded feed
  • Simple, clear composition — One clear focal point that reads instantly at thumbnail size
  • 16:9 aspect ratio — Add "--ar 16:9" to every thumbnail prompt in Midjourney
  • No text in the image — Add your text overlay in Photoshop, Canva, or Premiere Pro after generating the image, for better control over font and placement

Using Sora for Short Video Clips

Sora (OpenAI's video generation model) uses similar prompt logic to Midjourney but produces short video clips instead of still images. For Sora prompts, describe motion explicitly. Instead of just describing what is in the scene, describe how the camera moves and how objects move within the scene.

Example: "Slow cinematic drone shot slowly rising above a foggy mountain range at sunrise, golden light breaking through clouds, camera tilting upward, photorealistic, cinematic, 4K quality." Notice how the prompt describes the camera movement (rising, tilting upward) as well as the visual environment — this is crucial for getting good video results from Sora.
